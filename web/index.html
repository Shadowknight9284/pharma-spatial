How each product splits across indications

Part D has no diagnoses, so indication is inferred from prescriber specialty (e.g. a dermatologist's Dupixent fill → atopic dermatitis). Split rules are in seeds/specialty_indication.csv.

What this is

A public-data pipeline (Postgres + PostGIS) that builds a company × product × indication × prescriber × county × year cube from CMS Medicare Part D and Part B prescriber files, CMS Open Payments, NPPES, FDA NDC, CDC PLACES, CDC SVI and the Census ACS. This site shows county- and company-level aggregates only; no individual prescribers are named.

Metrics

Fills per 1,000 aged 65+
Part D 30-day-equivalent fills ÷ county population 65+ (ACS 5-year).
Promotion $
Open Payments general payments (meals, speaker fees, travel, consulting…) naming the product. A payment naming several products is split equally.
Promotion vs sales
County share of the product's national payments ÷ its share of national fills, smoothed toward 1 by adding 1% of the product's national payments to both sides. >1 means promoted more than its sales would predict.
Promotion vs burden
Same, against the county's share of disease burden (CDC PLACES prevalence × population; 65+ population where PLACES has no matching measure).
Share of class
Product's share of 30-day fills within its competitive class in that county.

Caveats

  • Medicare only (mostly 65+); no commercial or Medicaid prescriber-level data exists publicly.
  • CMS suppresses prescriber×drug cells under 11 claims: rural HCPs and new or rare drugs are undercounted (Dupixent's prescriber file covers ~83% of its Part D spend; Eliquis ~96%).
  • Indication is inferred from specialty, not observed.
  • Gross cost is before rebates. Open Payments excludes rep salaries, samples and DTC advertising.
  • Promotion targets high prescribers, so none of these associations are causal.
  • Puerto Rico and the territories are in the data but not drawn on the map.
